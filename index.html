2024
Senior thesis Urban policy

How Crime Relates to Investment and Disinvestment in Residential Properties: A Baltimore City Case Study PDF ↗

B.A. thesis, Public Policy Studies, The University of Chicago, April 2024. Awarded with Honors.

Abstract

Urban communities suffer from a spiral of decay where poor conditions induce crime, and crime contributes to worsening conditions. Policies aim to aid blighted areas through investment; however, the literature overlooks how residents themselves invest and disinvest in their homes in relation to surrounding crime trends. This study uses panel data on crime and housing indicators from the Baltimore Police Department and the Baltimore Department of Housing and Community Development to model the relationship between crime and investment in residential properties in Baltimore City, Maryland, from 2015 to 2021. Contrary to the expected narrative, the study finds a significant increase in rehabilitation projects within higher crime areas, suggesting an unexpected pattern of investment. However, these areas also see fewer demolitions than anticipated and maintain a higher proportion of unoccupied homes, suggesting that investment and disinvestment can coexist. The policy implications for Baltimore’s crime and vacancy problems are discussed.
